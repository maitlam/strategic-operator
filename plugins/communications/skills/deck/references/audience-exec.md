# Audience profile: exec

Leadership, sponsors, a steering group. They have the authority to decide and
fifteen minutes to do it. They will read the first slide, the ask, and whatever
is between them if the first slide earned it.

## Sequence

| # | Slide type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `title` | Purpose line says what decision this deck is for |
| 2 | `answer` | The conclusion as a sentence; the reasons under it |
| 3–7 | `point` (+ `evidence` only if load-bearing) | Three to five points, in order of strength. Evidence goes to the appendix unless a number *is* the point |
| 8 | `options` | Only if a real choice is being made |
| 9 | `ask` | Named decider, date, consequence |
| 10 | `next` | Owners and dates |
| — | `section` "Appendix" then `appendix` × n | Every evidence slide that didn't make the cut; methodology; caveats |

## Cap

**Ten main slides.** Appendix unlimited.

## Tone rules

- Answer first. If slide 2 isn't the conclusion, the deck is a document.
- Every headline is a full sentence stating a claim the exec could repeat in the next meeting.
- No process narrative. "We interviewed 30 customers" is appendix. "Customers churn in the first 30 days because onboarding assumes a data engineer" is the point.
- One number per point where possible; the number carries the claim.
- Caveats are one line, on the slide they qualify. Longer caveats go to the appendix.
- Nothing the exec can't act on. A finding with no implication is cut.

## Strip

- Methodology, sample sizes, research process
- Full data tables (move to appendix, keep one number on the point)
- Team-internal vocabulary, ticket names, codenames
- Anything under discussion that hasn't reached a recommendation
- Multiple options when one is recommended — show the recommendation, list alternatives in a line

## Add

- An `ask` slide, always — even if the ask is "endorse this direction"
- A `next` slide with owners and dates
- A one-line "what we're not doing" on the answer slide when scope was cut

## Test

Cover every slide except the `answer` and the `ask`. Does the exec have enough to decide? If not, the two slides aren't carrying enough; fix those before touching the middle.
