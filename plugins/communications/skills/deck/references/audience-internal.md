# Audience profile: internal

The working team, close collaborators, the people who will do the work or
whose work this changes. They need the reasoning, the disagreements, the
unknowns, and what's expected of them next.

## Sequence

| # | Slide type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `title` | |
| 2 | `answer` | Still first — the team also deserves the conclusion up front |
| 3 | `point` "Context" | Why this work happened; what question it set out to answer |
| 4 | `agenda` | If more than eight main slides |
| 5–n | `section` → `point` → `evidence` | Show the work. Each section is one line of reasoning; each point has its evidence directly behind it |
| n+1 | `point` "Where the evidence disagrees" | Contradictions and unresolved tensions, stated plainly |
| n+2 | `point` "What we don't know" | Open questions and what would resolve them |
| n+3 | `point` "What we're not doing" | Scope that was cut, and why |
| n+4 | `ask` or `next` | What's expected of the room |
| — | Appendix | Full data, sources, methodology |

## Cap

**Twenty-five main slides.** More than that and it's two decks.

## Tone rules

- Show the reasoning. The team will act on this longer than the exec will; they need to be able to reconstruct why.
- Disagreements and unknowns are content, not noise. A team deck that hides uncertainty produces surprised teammates later.
- Evidence sits next to the point it supports, not in the appendix.
- Internal vocabulary is fine. Codenames, ticket references, and system names are precise here.
- Sources on every evidence slide — the team will go check.

## Strip

- Nothing structural. This is the full version.
- Polish that costs time: the team would rather have the deck today.

## Add

- "Where the evidence disagrees" — always, even if the answer is "it doesn't"
- "What we don't know" — always
- "What we're not doing" — when scope was cut
- Per-person or per-workstream implications when the deck changes someone's work

## Test

Could a teammate who missed the meeting read this deck and both understand the conclusion and argue with it? If they can only understand, the reasoning isn't on the slides.
