# Audience profile: external

Partners, customers, vendors, another organization's leadership. They don't
share your context, your vocabulary, or your incentives, and this deck will
circulate inside their organization without you in the room.

## Sequence

| # | Slide type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `title` | Both organizations named; purpose line says what this conversation is for |
| 2 | `answer` | Framed as the shared opportunity or the proposal — what this is, in their terms |
| 3 | `point` "Why this matters to you" | Their problem, their outcome, in their words where possible |
| 4–8 | `point` (+ `evidence` where public) | The case, framed as mutual value. Three to five points |
| 9 | `point` "What we'd need from you" | Explicit, so nobody discovers it in the contract |
| 10 | `ask` | The next step being proposed — a pilot, a working session, a decision |
| 11 | `next` | Proposed timeline, who on each side |
| 12 | `point` "Contact" | Who to talk to, on your side |
| — | Appendix | Only what's cleared to share |

## Cap

**Twelve main slides.**

## Tone rules

- Their outcome before your capability. Lead with what changes for them.
- No internal vocabulary: no codenames, no org-chart references, no ticket names, no acronyms that aren't theirs.
- No third parties named unless the information is public — competitor names, other partners, customer names.
- Numbers are ones you'd be comfortable seeing quoted back. If it's an estimate, say so on the slide.
- Plain about what you need. Partnerships fail on the asks that were softened.
- Assume the deck is forwarded. Every slide stands alone and every slide is one you'd want them to see.

## Strip

- Internal metrics, targets, and plans not cleared for sharing
- Anything confidential about third parties
- Internal disagreements, open questions, "what we don't know"
- Methodology and process unless it's the product
- Org names, codenames, team structures

## Add

- "Why this matters to you" — always, and early
- "What we'd need from you" — always, and explicit
- A confidentiality footer (the `external` theme class adds one; adjust the wording to your agreement)
- A contact slide

## Test

Read every slide as if you were their general counsel and their skeptic. Is there anything you wouldn't want quoted, forwarded, or held against you? Is the ask something they could say yes to in the room?
