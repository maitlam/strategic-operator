# Audience profile: board

Directors, investors, an advisory board. They see the business a few times a
year, compare this quarter to last, and read for two things: is the plan on
track, and what do you need from them. They will notice anything that changed
shape since the last deck.

## Sequence

| # | Slide type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `title` | Period covered, not just a date |
| 2 | `answer` | The state of the business in one sentence, and whether the plan holds |
| 3 | `evidence` "Scorecard" | The same metrics as last time, same order: actual, target, trend, last period. This slide's shape never changes between decks |
| 4–5 | `point` | What moved and why — at most two. One number each |
| 6 | `point` "Risks" | Named, with likelihood, impact, and what's being done. Plain language; no risk is "being monitored" without saying what would trigger action |
| 7 | `ask` | What the board is being asked to approve, advise on, or note — one of the three, said which |
| 8 | `next` | Milestones to the next meeting |
| — | Appendix | Full financials, detail behind any scorecard line, anything requested last time |

## Cap

**Eight main slides.** The scorecard does the work a longer deck would.

## Tone rules

- Same format every time. Comparability across quarters is the point; a redesigned deck reads as something to hide.
- Numbers carry trend and target, always. A number alone is an anecdote.
- Risks in plain language with a trigger. "Hiring risk — if two of four open roles aren't filled by [date], [plan] slips a quarter."
- No surprises in the deck that weren't pre-wired. If something material happened, the chair heard before the deck landed; the deck confirms.
- Say what kind of ask it is: approve, advise, or note. Boards act differently for each.

## Strip

- Operational detail and process narrative
- Anything that changed only because it was reworded
- Selective metrics — a metric that appears when it's good and disappears when it isn't is noticed
- Optimism without a number

## Add

- Scorecard with unchanged shape — always
- Risks slide — always, even when the answer is "unchanged since last quarter"
- The ask, typed as approve / advise / note
- "Since last meeting" line on the answer slide when something material moved

## Test

Put this deck next to the last one. Can a director see what changed in under a minute? Is every metric that was on the last scorecard still on this one?
